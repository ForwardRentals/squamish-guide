01 · Things to do

Six ways to spend a day.

Squamish is a town people move to for one sport and stay for five. Here's where to start.

Climb

Squamish is one of the great granite climbing towns in the world. The Chief, Smoke Bluffs and Murrin Park hold thousands of routes, from family-friendly crags to big walls.

Murrin Park guide →

Ride

Hundreds of kilometres of mountain bike trail in Alice Lake, Diamond Head and Valleycliffe, built and kept up by local volunteers. Ride within your level: a lot of it is steep.

Squamish outdoors →

Paddle

Lakes for swimming, a tidal estuary for birds and seals, and Howe Sound for the bigger days. Rent a canoe or take the water taxi to trailheads you can't drive to.

Canoe rentals →

Hike

From the three peaks of the Chief to the falls at Shannon and the huts at Elfin Lakes and Watersprite, there is a trail for every fitness level.

Chief guide →

Watch

The Skwelwil'em Squamish Estuary is a short walk from downtown. In winter, bald eagles gather along the rivers near Brackendale to feed on spawning salmon.

Estuary guide →

Unwind

A sauna by the river, a pint at one of the local breweries and, on lucky winter nights, northern lights over the Chief.

02 · Seasons

When to come.

Squamish is a four-season town, but each season has a very different personality.

Spring

Waterfalls at full roar, the first dry days at the crags and quiet trails. Expect rain between the sunny spells.

Summer

Swim lakes, climbing, the Squamish Spit busy with kiteboarders on windy afternoons, and long evenings. Book rentals and camping early.

Fall

Salmon run in the rivers, fewer crowds and golden light on the Chief. The rain returns by November.

Winter

Bald eagles near Brackendale, moody fjord mornings, and Whistler's ski hills 45 minutes up the road.

04 · Around town

Neighbourhoods & nearby.

Squamish spreads up the valley from the water. A quick orientation:

  • DowntownThe walkable core by the water: cafés, shops, the estuary trails and the Spit road.
  • ValleycliffeSouth of downtown, under the Chief, with direct access to its trails and the Smoke Bluffs.
  • Garibaldi HighlandsUp the hill with big views and mountain bike trails out the back door.
  • BrackendaleNorth along the river: quieter, more rural, and the winter eagle-watching hub.
  • Britannia Beach15 minutes south, home of the Britannia Mine Museum.
  • Sea to Sky GondolaJust south of the Chief, with summit trails, viewing platforms and a suspension bridge.
  • Porteau CoveA provincial park on Howe Sound for diving, camping and sunsets.
  • Whistler45 minutes north for lifts, the Village and the alpine lakes.

06 · Getting here

On the Sea to Sky Highway.

Squamish sits on Highway 99, roughly an hour north of Vancouver and 45 minutes south of Whistler. The drive along Howe Sound is one of the most scenic in Canada, and one of the most demanding: it is winding, busy on weekends and needs winter tires from October through March.

Regional buses run between Vancouver, Squamish and Whistler. Once you are here, a car or bike makes the trailheads much easier to reach.

Conditions on trails, lakes and the highway change. Check BC Parks, DriveBC and local advisories before you head out.
